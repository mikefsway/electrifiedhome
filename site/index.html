Work it out for your home

The home energy helper takes you through the checks on this site one question at a time: what a heat pump, solar panels or a battery would cost and save, when to replace your boiler, whether your radiators would work with a heat pump, and which grants you might get. Your answers carry over from one check to the next.

Each check takes a few minutes, and none ask for your name or email. How the helper works, and who sees your answers

Where to start

Heating, hot water, cooking and the car can each switch to electricity on its own, and most homes do it over several years. The order below suits most homes, but it's a suggestion. Heating comes first because it uses most of the energy in a typical home.

  1. Plan the heating before the boiler fails

    A heat pump replaces a gas, oil or LPG boiler and heats your hot water too. It takes a few weeks to survey, design and fit, and a boiler that breaks in January is usually replaced with another boiler that week. The boiler escape plan works out when to switch.

  2. Check which grants you could get

    Grants cover some or all of the cost for many households, depending on where you live, your income and whether you own your home. Paying for it describes each scheme, and the grant finder shows which you might get today.

  3. Move to a tariff with cheaper hours

    Once the heating or car runs on electricity, the tariff makes the biggest difference to running costs. Most tariffs with cheaper hours need a smart meter.

  4. Change the hob when it suits you

    It doesn't depend on anything else, and a plug-in ring lets you try induction first.

  5. Add solar panels and a battery

    If your roof gets sun, panels and a battery cut the electricity you buy, which matters more once the heating runs on it.

  6. Charge the car at home

    When you next change car, charging on the drive is usually the cheapest way to run it.

  7. Choose installers carefully

    Get at least two quotes for a heat pump, solar panels or a battery. Choosing an installer says what a quote should include and what to ask.

Draught-proofing and topping up the loft help whatever heats the home, and you don't need to finish insulating before anything else. Your home's energy performance certificate (EPC) records the heating and insulation an assessor found. Search the register for England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland.
